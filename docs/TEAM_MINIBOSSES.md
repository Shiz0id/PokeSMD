# Adding a team to the mini boss roster

What Team Rocket (Gen 1 and Gen 2) and Team Galactic each still need before they
can stand on a mini boss floor, measured against the tree rather than guessed.
Written as prep, so the work does not start by re-deriving all of this.

Sits beside `KANTO_LEADERS.md` and `JOHTO_SINNOH_SPRITES.md`, which are the
precedents for porting a trainer set and vendoring the art for one.

## The roster today

`sRogueDungeonMiniBosses` in `include/constants/rogue_dungeon_trainers.h`,
generated by `tools/rogue/gen_trainer_table.py`. **116 entries, levels 9–54** —
Team Aqua and Team Magma (63, to 43) plus Gen 1 Team Rocket (53, to 54).

A mini boss is picked by depth, like the ordinary pool, so **what a team is worth
to the roster is the range it covers, not the count**. Aqua and Magma alone
stopped at 43 while the last gym dungeon asks for 45 — and before Archie and
Maxie were included they stopped at 38, which left the floor-75 mini boss seven
levels light. Rocket's 11–54 is why it was the one to port first; the ceiling is
no longer the roster's weak point.

Aqua and Magma grunts sit in **both** tables — 54 of them in the ordinary pool as
well as 63 in the roster. That is deliberate: `EXCLUDE_CLASSES` drops the admin
and leader classes from the random pool but not the plain grunts, so an Aqua
grunt can be either an ordinary floor trainer or a mini boss. Do not "fix" it.

**Rocket is in the roster only**, because `EXCLUDE_ID_PATTERNS` drops
`^TRAINER_ROGUE_` from the ordinary pool and every ported trainer carries that
prefix. Both behaviours are correct and they differ; see the Gen 1 section.

## THE GATE IS THE TRAINER ID BUDGET, NOT THE ART

Read this before planning any of the three.

```
TRAINERS_COUNT_EMERALD      960     (was 907; Gen 1 Rocket took 907-959)
MAX_TRAINERS_COUNT_EMERALD 1040     (was 920)
                            ----
free ids                      80
```

**The ceiling was raised and Gen 1 Rocket is in.** It was thirteen free ids
before, against 53 candidates for Rocket alone and another 50+ each for Gen 2
Rocket and Galactic, so curating all three into thirteen was not a plan. The
eighty left are earmarked for those two — enough for both if neither takes its
whole roster.

The cost of the raise is measured below rather than guessed, and the measurement
is the reason it was an easy call.

### Raising it by 120 costs 20 KB of ROM and 12 bytes of save — MEASURED

Built both ways, `MAX_TRAINERS_COUNT_EMERALD` 920 against 1040, same tree:

| | 920 | 1040 | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| ROM | 29,231,888 | 29,252,128 | **+20,240 B** |
| EWRAM | 246,028 | 246,040 | **+12 B** |
| IWRAM | 19,444 | 19,444 | 0 |
| `sizeof(SaveBlock1)` | 14,000 | 14,012 | +12 B, against a **15,872** cap |

**THE ENTIRE ROM COST IS ONE SYMBOL, AND IT IS NOT THE TRAINERS.** `gTrainers`
does not move — it is sized by `TRAINERS_COUNT`, the real count, not by the
ceiling. What grows is `sTrainerSlides` in `src/trainer_slide.c`:

```c
static const u8* const sTrainerSlides[DIFFICULTY_COUNT]
                                     [TRAINER_PARTNER(PARTNER_COUNT)]
                                     [TRAINER_SLIDE_COUNT] = { ... };
```

3 difficulties × 14 slide kinds × 4 bytes = **168 bytes of ROM per trainer id**,
paid whether or not that id exists, in a table that is almost entirely NULL. It
is 175 KB today. **If ROM ever becomes the constraint, that table is where 100+
KB is hiding** — making it sparse is a real option and nobody has needed it yet.

**The flag side is not the wall it looks like.** 120 ids cost 12 bytes of
SaveBlock1, leaving **1,860 bytes free** of the 15,872 the four save sectors
allow. At that rate the save block would take *thousands* more ids before
`SaveBlock1FreeSpace` fired. Flags are not what limits this; they never were.

**Then the trainers themselves cost ROM when they actually arrive**, at
`sizeof(struct Trainer)` = **52 bytes × DIFFICULTY_COUNT (3)** = 156 B each, so
120 real trainers add **~18.7 KB** to `gTrainers`, plus their party arrays.

**PARTIES ARE EMITTED ONCE, NOT PER DIFFICULTY** — settled by measuring the
Rocket port rather than left open. Adding 53 trainers moved ROM by 34,232 bytes
over the pre-raise baseline, of which 20,160 is `sTrainerSlides` (the ceiling)
and 8,268 is `gTrainers` (53 × 52 × 3, exactly as predicted). The remaining
**5,804 bytes is the party data — about 110 bytes per trainer**, which is one
copy, not three.

**Total for the whole plan — ceiling plus 120 trainers plus parties — is under
50 KB, against 4.12 MB of free ROM.** ROM is not the reason to hesitate.

### The reason to hesitate is unchanged

Raising it is possible and the arithmetic is already measured in
`include/constants/opponents.h` — 920 costs `NUM_FLAG_BYTES` 307, seven bytes of
SaveBlock1 over the original 864. **The cost is not the bytes, it is the
renumbering:** every system flag, badge flag and `FLAG_ROGUE_*` alias shifts, so
a save written before the change reads its bits at the old offsets. That is a
save-invalidating change and should be **batched with another one**, not spent
on its own. Land any raise on a byte boundary — 876 and 880 cost the same 302.

> The note at `opponents.h:878` saying "there are 57 spare" is **stale**; it was
> written before the Johto and Sinnoh ports. It is 13. Corrected in place.

## Team Rocket, Gen 1 — DONE

**53 trainers, ids 907–959, levels 11–54, in the roster.** The mini boss roster
went from 63 entries topping out at 43 to **116 topping out at 54**, which
closes the gap that made the floor-75 mini boss seven levels light.

Parties are generated by **`tools/rogue/gen_rocket_parties.py`**, which reads
`src/data/trainers_frlg.party` and re-emits the blocks verbatim under ids that
exist in this build. Nothing is scaled — a mini boss is picked BY DEPTH from a
roster sorted on level, so stock levels are the axis, not an error to correct.
`--check` proves `trainers.party` still matches the FRLG source; `--ids`
re-derives the `#define` block; `--print` re-emits the parties.

**They are mini bosses only, not ordinary floor trainers**, because
`EXCLUDE_ID_PATTERNS` drops `^TRAINER_ROGUE_` from the random pool while
`parse_team` does not apply that filter. Aqua and Magma grunts are in both lists;
Rocket is in one. That follows the documented rule — every `TRAINER_ROGUE_*`
exists because some table already owns it — and is worth knowing before someone
reads the asymmetry as a bug.

### THREE OF THEM ARE `Gender: Male` WITH A FEMALE BATTLE PIC

`GRUNT_42`, `GRUNT_51` and `ADMIN_1` carry `Pic: Rocket Grunt F Frlg` while the
`Gender` field says Male. In FireRed that field drives encounter music and costs
nothing visible, so the mismatch never showed there. Here it would have decided
the figure standing on the floor — a man on the tile who opens the battle as a
woman, which is the fault `check_theme_trainers.py` was written for after a
diver did exactly that.

`team_gfx` now takes the **pic** and lets it decide, with `Gender` as the
fallback for a pic that names no sex. Every Aqua and Magma row agrees either
way, so the change moved exactly three rows and left the other 63 byte-identical.

## Team Rocket, Gen 1 — what it needed (kept for the next port)

| piece | state |
|---|---|
| trainer class | `TRAINER_CLASS_TEAM_ROCKET_FRLG` — **present**, shared enum |
| battle pics | `TRAINER_PIC_ROCKET_GRUNT_M_FRLG` / `_F_FRLG` — **present and compiled** |
| overworld sprites | `OBJ_EVENT_GFX_ROCKET_M` / `_F` — **present**, real 16×32 animate people |
| class → sprite row | **done** — added to `sTrainerClassGfx` in `src/rogue_dungeon.c` |
| `TEAM_GFX` row | **written, commented out** in `gen_trainer_table.py` |
| parties | **missing — this is the whole job** |

**THE PICS COMPILE BUT THE TRAINERS DO NOT, AND THAT IS THE TRAP.**
`src/data/trainers_frlg.party` holds **53 Rocket trainers at levels 11–54**,
which is exactly the set wanted. They are not reachable. `gTrainers` in
`src/data.c` is:

```c
const struct Trainer gTrainers[DIFFICULTY_COUNT][TRAINERS_COUNT] =
{
#if IS_FRLG
#include "data/trainers_frlg.h"
#else
#include "data/trainers.h"
#endif
};
```

One table or the other, never both. This is an Emerald build, so every trainer in
`trainers_frlg.party` is **source material, not a trainer** — it has no id in
this build and referencing one does not compile. The trainer *pic* table is not
gated the same way, which is why the art is available while the data is not, and
why this looks more finished than it is.

**The route** is the one Johto and Sinnoh took: a
`tools/rogue/gen_rocket_parties.py` alongside `gen_johto_parties.py` and
`gen_sinnoh_parties.py`, emitting `TRAINER_ROGUE_ROCKET_*` blocks into
`trainers.party`, with `trainers_frlg.party` as the source to read levels,
species and movesets out of. **Parties are generated, never hand-edited into
`trainers.party`** — that rule is stated in `JOHTO_SINNOH_SPRITES.md` and applies
here.

Then uncomment the `TEAM_GFX` row and regenerate. Nothing else is needed: the
roster picks up any class in that table.

## Team Rocket, Gen 2 — parties only, plus four faces

The GSC uniform is the same, so the Gen 1 pics and overworld sprites cover the
grunts and no new art is needed for them. Once `gen_rocket_parties.py` exists,
Gen 2 grunts are more rows through the same tool.

**The executives are the exception.** Ariana, Petrel, Proton and Archer have no
pics and no overworld sprites anywhere in the tree. They need the
`JOHTO_SINNOH_SPRITES.md` route — vendored third-party art, credited in that
document — the same as Johto's ten new leaders did.

## Team Galactic — art first

| piece | state |
|---|---|
| battle music | **present** — `MUS_DPPT_BATTLE_TEAM_GALACTIC` (619), `MUS_DPPT_BATTLE_GALACTIC_ADMIN` (819), imported with the DPPt pack |
| trainer class | missing — needs an enum entry |
| battle pics | missing |
| overworld sprites | missing |
| parties | missing |

Nothing but the music. This is the expensive one and it is an **art job before it
is a data job**: grunt M/F pics, grunt M/F overworld sprites, and whichever of
Mars, Jupiter, Saturn and Cyrus are wanted. Follow `JOHTO_SINNOH_SPRITES.md` for
sourcing and credit; that document is the reference for how sprites are vendored
here, not this one.

Having the music already is worth more than it sounds — a mini boss theme that
matches the team is otherwise its own small project.

## Recipe

1. **Decide the id budget first.** Curate to 13, or raise
   `MAX_TRAINERS_COUNT_EMERALD` and accept the save invalidation, batched.
2. Art, if the team needs it — `JOHTO_SINNOH_SPRITES.md`.
3. A trainer class enum entry, if the team needs one.
4. `gen_*_parties.py` emitting `TRAINER_ROGUE_*` blocks into `trainers.party`.
5. A row in `sTrainerClassGfx` (`src/rogue_dungeon.c`) so the overworld figure
   matches the battle pic.
6. A row in `TEAM_GFX` (`gen_trainer_table.py`), then regenerate.
7. Run `verify_run_structure.py` — mini boss levels are checked against the
   curve, and a set running to 54 changes the ceiling it is measured against.

## Traps already paid for

**`team_gfx` used to guess.** It ended in
`team = 'MAGMA' if 'Magma' in cls else 'AQUA'`, so any team added to the roster
that was not Magma came out in an **Aqua uniform** — walking the floor as a Team
Aqua grunt while opening the battle with its own trainer pic. A real sprite, a
real enum member, a clean build, nothing to see. It is now a table, `TEAM_GFX`,
and an unknown class stops the tool. **Adding a team is a row, not a code edit.**

**An unfilled `sTrainerClassGfx` row is not an error either.** It falls back to
the theme's own trainer sprite, so the team's mini boss walks around as a hiker.
That table is also where `TRAINER_CLASS_RS_PROTAG` once named the Ruby and
Sapphire *gemstones* instead of the RS protagonists — see the changelog. Check
what a symbol actually is before naming it; `check_trainer_pool_sanity.py`
asserts every row is an animate person, which is what catches that class of
mistake now.
